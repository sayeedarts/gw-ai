BEYOND CLASSICAL PHYSICS

UNDERSTANDING THE QUANTUM WORLD.

Classical physics describes many familiar physical phenomena at everyday scales. However, at atomic and subatomic scales, matter and energy exhibit behaviours that require a different mathematical and conceptual framework.

Quantum physics provides this framework. It introduces principles such as quantisation, superposition, uncertainty and entanglement, which help explain the behaviour of microscopic systems.

These principles have also contributed to the development of modern technologies, including semiconductors, lasers and quantum information systems.

THE SCIENCE OF QUANTUM MECHANICS

FOUR PRINCIPLES THAT DEFINE THE QUANTUM WORLD.

01. Quantisation

Certain physical properties, such as energy in specific quantum systems, can take discrete values rather than varying continuously.

02. Superposition

A quantum system can exist in a mathematical combination of multiple possible states until a measurement is made.

03. Quantum Entanglement

Two or more quantum systems can share correlated states that cannot be fully described independently, even when separated spatially.

04. Uncertainty Principle

Certain pairs of physical properties, such as position and momentum, cannot simultaneously have arbitrarily precise values.

FROM PHYSICS TO COMPUTATION

THE SCIENCE BEHIND QUANTUM INFORMATION.

Quantum information science studies how information can be represented, manipulated and processed using quantum mechanical systems.

01

Qubits

The fundamental units of quantum information, represented through quantum states.

02

Quantum Gates

Operations that manipulate quantum states.

03

Quantum Circuits

Arrangements of quantum operations designed to perform computational tasks.

04

Quantum Measurement

The process of extracting classical information from a quantum system.

EMERGING COMPUTATIONAL PARADIGMS

A DIFFERENT APPROACH TO COMPUTATION.

Quantum computing uses quantum mechanical principles to process information. Unlike conventional binary computing, which operates on classical bits, quantum computers use quantum bits or qubits.

Certain quantum algorithms may offer advantages for specific classes of computational problems. However, quantum computers are not universally faster or better than classical computers. Their advantages depend on the problem, algorithm and hardware implementation.
